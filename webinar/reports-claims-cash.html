Reports, Claims and Cash: what running one office and forty taught us

Live webinar · Thursday, December 10, 2026 · 12:00–1:00 pm Eastern · Google Meet · no fee · 0.1 AAA CEUs (pending Academy approval); IHS CE pending

Whether a practice has one chair or forty, three systems decide whether it runs smoothly and makes money: the report that goes back to the referring physician, the claim that goes out to the payer, and the numbers the owner reads. The instructor grew up in a three-generation hearing practice, helped build and run a multi-state group that reached more than 40 offices, and now owns a three-office practice. The course covers what those offices learned the hard way about each system: how late or missing reports cost referrals, and how a clinician can sign a complete report from a phone minutes after the visit instead of staying late or letting reports slide; how claims get lost between the chairside visit and the remittance, and the handful of measures that keep them visible; and the four numbers — cash position, weeks of runway, gross margin and net margin — read by office and by employee. It closes with a candid comparison of how a practice can run these systems today: by hand, with the practice-management systems in common use, or with a system built around them. The comparison names specific products, including one the instructor owns; see the content disclosure.

Upon completion, participants will be able to:

  1. Describe a report workflow that delivers a signed report to the referring provider within one business day of the visit, and name the three points where reports stall: test results typed from the printout, the clinician's writing time, and the delivery step.
  2. Define clean-claim rate, days in accounts receivable and the electronic remittance advice (ERA), and set up a weekly routine that surfaces any claim unpaid after 30 days and any deposit with no remittance behind it.
  3. Define cash position, weeks of runway, gross margin and net margin; read a profit-and-loss by office and a contribution view by employee; and name the staffing, location and marketing decisions each supports.
